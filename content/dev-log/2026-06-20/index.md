- It looks like it pitched up slightly and was starting to turn it bit towards to end. I tested a couple more times, but I think I'm reaching the limit of the data I can gather inside my apartment. It's time to test this outside!
- How will I actually test this? Right now I've been testing with my launcher placed on these steps, which total are 30-32cm off the ground. In the park, I won't have this extra height. And there might be some tall grass that interferes. This extra height really seems to be the perfect elevation for testing this. I wonder if I can bring some other platform to give my launcher some more height.
	- Ideas: boxes, fold up my table--can either place directly on the ground for like 10-20cm, or stand up for full height
- The other thing is wind. 10 seconds is a while, and any breeze will probably kill my glider. We'll see. I might be able to test at night to mitigate this, but it seems I'll have to deal with this to some degree.
- Supplies: computer + usb to reprogram power, plane, boxes for height, launcher, tape, screwdriver, extra launcher sled
- Separately, my edits to the printer settings to print the legs faster has made them weak enough that I need to hold the launcher down a bit so it doesn't wobble. To be fixed in the future.
- I tested in the park. It was really windy. Really hard to tell what was wind vs. actual performance. When I just made it to the park I realized the tail hot glue was pealing off. I had a weak rubber band on me that worked ish, but I realized I had also forgotten tape. Generally the park was pretty empty, but there were just enough people + dogs that it was difficult to get good tests in.
- It seemed the plane had a tendency to veer up and to the right. Though that's exactly what I would expect from the effect of the wind. I'm going to try again at night at a different location that I think will help a little bit here (or maybe go early morning). I don't know how else I will ever be able to test this.
