- Launcher time. Planning to first start by launching a stick, then will evolve that into a glider.
- I want something that sits on the ground, and launches something maybe less than 100g at say a 10 degree angle. Ideally this is robust enough to handle plenty of launches without wearing down, say at least 10 without any meaningful difference. 100g is just a rough estimate of a ceiling for my aircraft weight, 10 degree angle is loosely based off of pitch angle of light sport aircrafts (not necessarily representative of my small unmanned aircraft, but it's something), and 10 times is just based off feeling. I basically just don't want to have something like my rubber bands break during flight testing, which is a pretty low bar.
- I need to design a mechanism for this, and I'll figure out out how to adapt whatever holster for different aircraft designs down the line.
- First ideas right now is just something elastic like a spring or rubber band as the source of power. And I'd like something that I can pull back, click into place, plop down the aircraft, and press something to release it. Something like [this](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CJJWtcqeWnk).

- Design concept
	- 400mm 20x20 aluminum extrusion for the main rail structure. Pretty cheap, very rigid, comes with cavities that will be easy to attach a sled to, smooth/low friction against whatever plastic I use for the sled.
	- 1 3d printed slot at the front of the rail, with some hooks, for the...
	- 1 tension spring. I bought a kit of assorted ones. Can measure the force by hanging some known weights and measuring distance. Doesn't have to be exact. This will be used to pull the sled forward. Went with tension instead of compression since compression might require guard rails to prevent going sideways, whereas tension doesn't have that issue. Tension also makes the design slightly easier, in the the hook to hold/release the sled can apply force along the same line as the spring. This will be clearer with 3d models, but if say your spring pushes on the bottom of the sled, but your hook is on the top of the sled, when it's cocked, it'll want to rotate the sled, which might be fine but doesn't seem ideal.
	- 1 3d printed slot at the back of the rail. This needs to hook onto some ridge on the sled. Idea is that sled is pulled back, clicks into the hook here, then the hook is released via some mechanical action (likely pushing down on some opposing lever), and the sled rides the rail. So this will need to have some sort of basic hinge.
	- Sled will be custom printed to fit the extrusion + the plane. Most likely will push the plane forward by the back of the wings. Not entirely sure how this will work yet, but can be developed independent of the rest of the launcher for the most part I think.
	- 1 3d printed adapter to stand up the rail at an X degree angle.
